Information Systems for Business Management

An MBA-level course examining how Information Systems create business value, support managerial decision-making and enable enterprise integration. The course connects business analysis, requirements, MIS, DSS and ERP with real organizational problems and technology-enabled management.

Course Identity

Teaching Information Systems as a managerial decision discipline

The course is designed around the question of how managers evaluate, select and use Information Systems to solve organizational problems. Students move from digital-business foundations and system architecture into feasibility, stakeholder and requirement analysis, then examine how MIS, DSS and ERP support operational performance, managerial decisions and enterprise-wide integration.

Learning Focus

MBA students are guided to:

  • Explain how Information Systems support digital business and organizational performance.
  • Analyse business problems before recommending technology-enabled solutions.
  • Evaluate feasibility, stakeholders and system requirements from a managerial perspective.
  • Interpret reports, dashboards and KPIs for managerial decision-making.
  • Distinguish MIS, DSS and different levels and structures of business decisions.
  • Evaluate ERP-enabled enterprise integration, implementation challenges and business value.
Teaching Approach

Frame → evaluate → decide → recommend

The course emphasizes managerial reasoning: students are expected to connect technology with an organizational need, compare alternatives and justify recommendations rather than discuss technology in isolation.

01 · Frame

Define the business problem

Cases begin with organizational goals, pain points, stakeholders and the business context in which an Information System is being considered.

02 · Evaluate

Assess the proposed solution

Students use feasibility, stakeholder and requirement analysis to examine whether a technology solution is appropriate and viable.

03 · Decide

Use information managerially

MIS, DSS, reports, dashboards and KPIs are interpreted in the context of operational, tactical and strategic decision-making.

04 · Recommend

Communicate a business position

Assignments, case discussions and presentations require students to explain benefits, risks, alternatives and managerial recommendations.

Graduate learning pathway

01

Information Systems & Digital Business

Establishing the strategic role of Information Systems in contemporary organizations.

  • Information Systems in modern business
  • Traditional versus digital business
  • Digital transformation
  • Data, information and knowledge
  • Information quality
  • Business value and managerial decision support
02

Architecture, Feasibility & Business Analysis

Connecting organizational needs with the structure and viability of technology-enabled solutions.

  • Information System components and layered architecture
  • On-premise versus cloud deployment
  • Business problems versus technology solutions
  • TELOS feasibility analysis
  • Stakeholder identification and analysis
  • Role of the Business Analyst
03

Requirements Engineering for Business

Translating organizational needs into clearer, measurable and manageable system requirements.

  • Requirements elicitation
  • Interviews, observation, surveys and workshops
  • Brainstorming, focus groups and document analysis
  • Prototyping
  • Ambiguity and conflict analysis
  • Business, user, functional and non-functional requirements
  • Specification, validation and management
04

MIS, DSS & Managerial Decision-Making

Understanding how organizational information is transformed into managerial insight and decision support.

  • Management Information Systems and TPS
  • Management reports and business dashboards
  • Key Performance Indicators
  • Operational, tactical and strategic decisions
  • Structured, semi-structured and unstructured decisions
  • Decision Support Systems
  • Alternatives, risk and managerial judgment
05

ERP, Enterprise Integration & Emerging Systems

Examining integrated enterprise systems as organizational infrastructure rather than isolated software applications.

  • ERP foundations, architecture and functional modules
  • Cross-functional enterprise processes
  • ERP implementation and governance
  • Business value and implementation challenges
  • OLTP and OLAP
  • Cloud ERP and AI-enabled ERP
  • Business Analytics and future enterprise Information Systems
