Course Identity

Teaching students to analyse systems before designing solutions

The course develops a structured approach to information-system analysis. Students learn to move from an organizational problem to a clearly defined scope, assess feasibility, identify and refine stakeholder requirements, model system processes and examine how testing supports system quality and reliability.

Learning Focus

Students are guided to:

  • Analyse organizational systems and identify underlying business problems.
  • Apply SDLC thinking to structured system-development decisions.
  • Evaluate technical, economic and operational feasibility.
  • Identify, refine and structure functional and non-functional requirements.
  • Model business processes using Context Diagrams and Data Flow Diagrams.
  • Apply verification, validation and testing concepts to system analysis.
Teaching Approach

Understand → analyse → specify → model → validate

The course is taught through structured explanations, real organizational cases, analytical exercises and system-modelling practice so students learn to justify each stage of an information-system solution.

01 · Understand

Study the organization

Students first identify the system context, actors, processes and business problem before proposing change.

02 · Analyse

Evaluate the problem

Scope, feasibility, stakeholders and competing needs are examined through structured case analysis.

03 · Specify

Refine requirements

Informal needs are translated into clearer functional and non-functional requirements while ambiguity and conflicts are examined.

04 · Model

Visualise system flow

Context Diagrams and Level-0 DFDs are used to represent processes, external entities, data stores and data movement.

05 · Validate

Question system quality

Verification, validation, test cases and defect analysis are introduced to connect system analysis with quality assurance.

Curriculum Architecture

Course learning pathway

The public portfolio groups the course into analytical capabilities rather than reproducing internal schedules, examination weeks or administrative course details.

01

Systems Thinking & SDLC

Understanding organizations as systems and why structured development processes are needed.

  • Information systems and organizational systems
  • System components, types and characteristics
  • Problems with ad-hoc development
  • System Development Life Cycle
  • Planning, analysis, design and implementation phases
  • Real-world system-development scenarios
02

Feasibility & Requirements Engineering

Moving from a business problem to a feasible and clearly specified system scope.

  • Problem statements and scope definition
  • Technical, economic and operational feasibility
  • Stakeholder and requirement analysis
  • Ambiguous and conflicting requirements
  • Functional and non-functional requirements
  • Requirement structuring and quality evaluation
03

Process Modelling with DFDs

Representing system boundaries, processes and data movement through structured models.

  • Process-modelling concepts
  • DFD symbols, rules and notation
  • Context Diagrams
  • Level-0 Data Flow Diagrams
  • Processes, data stores and external entities
  • Data-flow validation and model review
04

Testing & System Quality

Connecting analysis and design decisions with verification, validation and dependable system behaviour.

  • Introduction to system testing
  • Verification versus validation
  • Unit, integration, system and acceptance testing
  • Functional, negative and edge testing
  • Test cases and defect reporting
  • Boundary-oriented testing scenarios
05

Integrated System Analysis

Bringing together the full analytical process through project-based work on realistic organizational systems.

  • System problem identification and scope definition
  • Feasibility analysis
  • Requirement specification and refinement
  • Context Diagram and DFD development
  • Testing considerations and quality discussion
  • Presentation and justification of the proposed analysis
