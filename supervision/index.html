Research Supervision & Mentoring

Structured guidance from research question to publication

I supervise research through clear milestones, reproducible experimentation and evidence-based writing. The goal is to help researchers move from a promising idea to a defensible study with transparent methods, interpretable results and publication-ready outputs.

Supervision Philosophy

Research should be ambitious, testable and reproducible

My supervision style combines independent research with structured checkpoints. I encourage researchers to make their own technical decisions, but those decisions must be supported by clear research questions, appropriate baselines, transparent evaluation and careful interpretation of evidence.

Core Principles

What I emphasize during supervision

  • Define the contribution before expanding the implementation.
  • Verify datasets and experimental design before trusting results.
  • Use baselines, ablations and stress tests to support claims.
  • Document decisions so experiments can be reproduced.
  • Keep manuscript claims aligned with the evidence produced.
Mentoring Workflow

Question → design → evaluate → communicate

Research progress is organized around decision points rather than activity alone. Each stage should produce evidence that the study is ready to move forward.

01 · Question

Clarify the research gap

Define the problem, contribution, scope and relationship to prior work before committing to a large implementation.

02 · Design

Build a defensible study

Establish datasets, splits, baselines, metrics, comparison conditions and reproducible experimental procedures.

03 · Evaluate

Test more than headline performance

Use error analysis, robustness checks, ablation and failure cases to understand what the method actually contributes.

04 · Communicate

Write from the evidence

Structure the manuscript around the research question, method, results, limitations and defensible contribution.

Current Postdoctoral Supervision

Robustness testing for plant-disease classification

A current supervision project investigates how metamorphic testing can be used to evaluate and improve the robustness of image-classification models under realistic input transformations.

Research Focus

Metamorphic Testing for Robust Plant-Disease Classification

The study examines baseline deep-learning models and evaluates whether predictions remain stable under controlled transformations such as changes in lighting, blur, rotation, occlusion and background. The project combines robustness evaluation, failure analysis, ablation and reproducibility-oriented reporting.

Working Style

I use milestone-based supervision so progress can be evaluated against concrete outputs: literature synthesis, dataset audit, baseline results, experiment logs, ablation evidence, figures and manuscript sections. This helps research remain focused while still leaving room for independent technical judgment.
