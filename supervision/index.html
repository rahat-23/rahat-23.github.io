Supervision Philosophy

Strong research starts with the right question, not a preferred technique

My supervision approach is problem-driven and methodologically flexible. I encourage researchers to begin with a meaningful research question, understand the domain and evidence requirements, and then choose methods that are appropriate for the problem rather than forcing every study into a single technical area.

Core Principles

What I emphasize during supervision

  • Start from a clear problem, gap and contribution.
  • Choose methods that fit the research question and domain.
  • Build reproducible datasets, baselines and evaluation protocols.
  • Use robustness, ablation and error analysis where they strengthen the evidence.
  • Interpret results critically and communicate limitations clearly.
Mentoring Workflow

Question → design → evaluate → communicate

The workflow stays consistent across research domains even when the models, datasets and application areas change.

01 · Question

Define the contribution

Clarify the problem, research gap, scope and expected contribution before implementation expands.

02 · Design

Build the study

Select suitable datasets, methods, baselines, metrics and comparison conditions for the research question.

03 · Evaluate

Understand the result

Go beyond headline performance through comparative analysis, failure cases, robustness checks and appropriate statistical or empirical evaluation.

04 · Communicate

Develop the research narrative

Align the manuscript, figures, claims and limitations with the evidence produced by the study.

Current Research Example

Metamorphic Testing for Robust Plant-Disease Classification

The study investigates whether image-classification models remain reliable under controlled transformations such as lighting variation, blur, rotation, occlusion and background change. Baseline models, metamorphic relations, severity analysis, ablation and failure-guided improvement are used to evaluate robustness systematically.

Working Style

Structured progress with room for independent research

I use milestone-based supervision to keep research focused while allowing researchers to develop their own technical judgment. Progress is evaluated through concrete outputs such as literature synthesis, dataset audits, baseline results, experimental logs, comparative analyses, figures and manuscript sections.
